# Go-to-market: prove value with Kenyan agencies

## Positioning

**Estate AI Agent CRM helps a letting/property-management team respond consistently, remember client requirements, and avoid unverified property claims.** It is not a property portal, a legal verification service, or an autonomous transaction agent.

Lead with the operational result—not the word "AI":
- Fewer inquiries lost between WhatsApp and the team.
- Faster first response and clearer human handoffs.
- Less repeated asking of clients about budget, location, and requirements.
- Fewer stale/unsupported availability claims.
- A traceable queue for decisions that must stay human-approved.

## First ideal customer profile (ICP)

Start with **small and mid-sized Kenyan letting/property-management agencies** where a manager can make a decision quickly:
- Approximately 2–20 staff (validate this range in interviews).
- Repeated rental inquiries arriving through WhatsApp, calls, and web forms.
- Several active listings and one person coordinating viewings.
- A manager who can provide test listings and review outcomes.
- Currently uses spreadsheets, shared phones, or scattered chat threads.

Avoid large enterprise rollouts and handling real tenant personal data until authentication, tenant isolation, privacy controls, and recovery tests are complete.

## First customer segment

Start in Nairobi because public agency directories show a dense set of active agencies and property managers. Expand to other Kenyan towns only after the workflow is repeatable. Public directories are lead sources, not endorsements or proof that a company wants the product.

Possible discovery prospects (approach politely; ask for a short interview, not an immediate sale):

## The offer: a tightly scoped design-partner pilot

Recruit **3 agencies** for a 14-day pilot. Before the product is cleared for real customer data, use synthetic/sample data or a supervised demo with no real tenant identifiers.

Pilot scope:
1. Import 10–30 sample property records, with a named person responsible for verification.
2. Enter synthetic or explicitly consented test inquiries.
3. Demonstrate preference memory, verified-inventory responses, and human approval holds.
4. Compare the workflow against the agency's current process.
5. Review the results with the manager and agree whether it saved time or reduced missed follow-ups.

Do not promise integrations that are not implemented. Viewing requests must remain unbooked until a real calendar is connected. Approvals record a decision only; they do not send an offer, book a viewing, or move money.

## Discovery questions (ask before building more)

1. Walk me through the last 10 rental inquiries. Where did they arrive?
2. How many inquiries did you receive last week, and how many got no reply?
3. What takes the most time: replying, matching listings, confirming availability, arranging viewings, or following up?
4. How do you know a listing is still available today?
5. What happens when a staff member is away or leaves?
6. What software or spreadsheet do you already pay for?
7. What would have to improve for you to pay for a tool like this?
8. Who approves purchases and what budget range could they test?
9. What information would you refuse to put into an early product?
10. May I return with a prototype and ask you to judge it against your current process?

Do not lead the interview with "Would you use my AI?" Ask for examples of actual past behavior.

## Proof of value: baseline and pilot metrics

Capture a baseline for 5 working days, then track the same measures during the pilot:
- Median time to first response (minutes).
- Percentage of inquiries receiving a response within the agency's chosen target.
- Number of inquiries with a documented next step.
- Number of duplicate questions caused by lost context.
- Number of unsupported property/availability claims (target: zero).
- Number of requests correctly handed to a human.
- Staff minutes spent per inquiry (sampled, not guessed).
- Weekly active staff and repeat usage.
- Manager's willingness to continue at a stated price.

Record actual counts and the source. Never display invented "time saved", conversion rates, or revenue lift in the product or sales material.

## Pricing experiments (hypotheses, not established market prices)

Test simple options with actual decision-makers:
- Starter: KES 3,000–5,000 per agency/month for a small team and capped usage.
- Team: KES 8,000–15,000 per agency/month for multiple staff and reporting.
- Assisted onboarding: a one-time fee only if customers say setup is valuable.

Treat these as interview anchors, not final pricing. Ask what the current problem costs them, then test a paid continuation. Do not make the pilot free indefinitely.

## 30-day execution

### Days 1–3: customer discovery
- Contact 15 carefully selected agencies individually.
- Book at least 5 interviews.
- Document exact words, current workflow, and measurable pain.
- Do not add features until at least 3 interviews point to the same costly problem.

### Days 4–7: focused demo
- Prepare a clean demo dataset with clearly labelled sample records.
- Run 5 scripted scenarios: new inquiry, budget preference, no verified listing, viewing request, and offer requiring human approval.
- Show what the system will not do as clearly as what it can do.

### Days 8–21: pilot
- Onboard 3 design partners using sample/consented data only.
- Review usage and failures twice weekly.
- Ask staff to report wrong answers and missing handoffs.
- Fix the top three workflow blockers; avoid unrelated feature work.

### Days 22–30: conversion decision
- Present each pilot's baseline versus measured results.
- Ask for a paid continuation or a specific reason they will not pay.
- Continue only if at least 2 of 3 partners repeatedly use it and at least 1 agrees to pay (initial validation threshold, not a guarantee).
- If nobody pays, revisit the problem and customer segment rather than assuming more code will solve it.

## Path to a real business

1. One team uses it every workday.
2. It produces a measurable operational improvement.
3. A customer pays and renews.
4. Several similar agencies repeat the result without founder-heavy support.
5. Onboarding, support, privacy, reliability, and pricing become repeatable.
6. Expand by customer evidence, not by geography alone.

A large valuation is an outcome of sustained revenue, retention, trust, and distribution—not a feature checklist. The immediate goal is one customer who would be genuinely disappointed if the product disappeared.
